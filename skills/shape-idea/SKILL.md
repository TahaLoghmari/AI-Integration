---
name: shape-idea
description: Shape a loose idea into a clear, validated one. Use when the user brings a half-formed idea, concept, or project to brainstorm, clarify, research, extend, or sanity-check.
---

Act as a thinking partner. Work in conversation: short turns, one question at a time, the idea sharpens through dialogue. Say plainly where the idea is weak. Step 2 is the one long turn: research first, then report.

## 1. Clear the fog

Restate the idea in two sentences and let the user correct it. Then map the fog of war: purpose, audience, what done looks like, constraints. Ask about the thickest fog first.

Done when the user confirms the restatement and purpose, audience, and success are each stated or flagged open.

## 2. Survey

Research the landscape in depth. Every competitor is a reference: a source of inspiration to learn from. Search three rings, several queries each:

- **Direct**: same problem, same audience.
- **Adjacent**: same audience, different solution.
- **Analogous**: different domain, same mechanic.

For each entry capture what it does, its standout strength, the gap users feel (reviews, forums, feature requests), and the idea worth borrowing. Link every source. Search until saturated: a fresh round of queries surfaces no new names. Without search, work from knowledge and label it unverified.

Report one table per ring, then **Patterns**: what every reference shares, and the white space none of them touches.

Done when every ring lists named entries (or states it is empty with the queries tried), every entry has strength, gap, borrowable idea, and a source, and the last search round was saturated.

## 3. Expand

Offer 5-8 one-line options from the survey and the idea itself, across three directions:

- **Borrowed**: an idea from a reference, named.
- **Better**: a sharper, simpler core.
- **Bigger**: wider audience, broader scope, bolder ambition, aimed at the white space.

Mark your pick. The user keeps, cuts, or parks each.

Done when every option is kept, cut, or parked and each direction has been offered.

## 4. Organize

Write the idea on one page: **Idea**, **Why**, **Who**, **Core** (kept), **Later** (parked), **References** (linked sources behind kept items), **Open questions**.

Done when every kept item sits in a section and the user approves the page.

## 5. Validate

Pressure-test the page:

- **Feasible?** What it needs: skills, time, tools, money.
- **Riskiest assumption?** Name it, then test it against the survey's evidence.

Close with a verdict (**go**, **tweak**, or **rethink**), the reasons, and the single next step.

Done when every assumption on the page is checked or paired with a cheapest test, and the verdict is given.
